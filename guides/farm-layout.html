The first-week layout

A clean crop block, sprinklers in the right places, and a coop door facing the path so animals don't bottleneck. Total setup: about an in-game day.

Step 1. Clear the central plateau

The default farm starts with weeds and a few logs. Spend the first morning clearing a 24×12 tile zone in the upper-center of your land. This is your crop block.

Step 2. Plant the first crop block

Plant a mix of:

  • Spring: Parsnips, Cauliflower, Potatoes, Calflowers (best $/tile).
  • Summer: Melons, Blueberries (re-growing), Hops.
  • Fall: Pumpkins, Cranberries, Eggplant, Amaranth.

Use long rows (12 tiles) instead of squares. Long rows maximize sprinkler coverage.

Step 3. Place sprinklers before harvest

The basic sprinkler waters a 4-tile cross. The quality sprinkler waters 8 tiles. To hit a 12-tile row you'll want 3 basic sprinklers placed every 4 tiles along the centerline of the row.

  • Beginner budget: plant by hand until day 5, then buy 1 basic sprinkler from Pierre.
  • Mid-summer: upgrade to a quality sprinkler, one per row.
  • Late-game: Iridium Sprinkler waters 24 tiles — covers a full row at once.

Step 4. Coop and barn placement

Animal buildings should sit on the eastern side of the farm, with their door facing east toward a fenced animal yard.

  • Coop door east — chickens roam in the morning, don't block the path to the house.
  • Barn door east — same reasoning for cows and pigs.
  • Place a feeding bench within the fenced area, never outside, to avoid the AI walking away.
  • Hay silo goes south of both, near the shipping bin.

Step 5. Shipping bin near the path

Put the shipping bin within 2 tiles of the path so you can dump artisan goods in one click. A common mistake: shipping bin on the western side forces you to walk past the crop block twice a day.

Step 6. Reserve space for the silo and greenhouse

Even though the greenhouse unlocks later, leave 4×6 tiles clear on the south end of your farm now. That avoids the painful "I have to demolish sprinklers" moment at year-one end.

Common beginner layout mistakes

  • Crops next to paths: walking through crops tramples them. Keep a 2-tile buffer between crop blocks and any path you use.
  • Coop facing west: animals walk into your path in the morning and stall delivery routes.
  • Too many crops in year one: 8 rows is the maximum you'll water by hand. Anything more is wasted on a fresh save.
  • Scattered sprinklers: one sprinkler per 4 tiles in a row beats 8 sprinklers scattered randomly.

FAQ

When should I buy the first animal?

After you have a coop and a silo, both of which cost about 6000g combined. Aim for the end of spring before buying the first chicken.

Should I redesign the farm at year one end?

Most veterans do. The first-year layout exists to teach you the game's economy; year two lets you commit to a stable design.

Heads up: The above layout assumes the default farm map. If you picked a different farm shape in character creation, rotate the crop row direction — the principle (8 rows + 12 sprinklers + east-facing coop) stays the same.
